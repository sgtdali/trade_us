Idea-generation screen
I treated the uploaded pack as the authoritative numeric layer: canonical valuation/prices are as of August 11, 2026, consensus is August 12, 2026, and financial periods vary by issuer. I did not recompute or overwrite its growth, margins, cash flow, multiples, revisions, or targets. The mandate is long-only, common equity, no benchmark assumption, with weekly review/monthly rebalance.
The funnel is 5 A / 4 B / 3 C / 0 Reject. “A” means immediate deeper-research priority, not a buy recommendation. I am not forcing rejects because these 12 names already survived an earlier 87-company sector screen; C is where I put names whose apparent attractiveness is currently undermined by denominator quality, stale data, or insufficient risk compensation.
Highest-priority research candidates
ADBE — A, value plus monetization proof. The pack has Adobe at 14.5x P/E, 9.8% proxy FCF yield and 11.5x EV/reported operating income; all three sit at unusually inexpensive points versus its own history. Meanwhile revenue is still growing 12.3% and FCF margin is 38.6%. The missing qualitative piece is encouraging: Adobe said AI-first ARR more than tripled year over year to above $500 million and raised FY26 revenue and non-GAAP EPS targets. (Securities and Exchange Commission) The wedge is that the market may be pricing structural AI displacement while the installed base is beginning to monetize AI. The rejection test is attribution: Semrush contributed roughly $480 million of ARR, AI-first ARR is still small versus total ARR, and CEO/CFO transition increases execution risk. (Securities and Exchange Commission) I would advance it only if the next work can show organic AI monetization and durable margins rather than acquisition-assisted growth.
AMZN — A, capex-backed acceleration rather than capex destruction. The pack shows 18.2% revenue growth, 36.6% operating-profit growth and 44.2% OCF growth, but negative FCF and capex at 25.8% of revenue. The external evidence strengthens the numerator: AWS accelerated to 36.7% growth, its fastest in 18 quarters, while both the AI and chip businesses exceeded $25 billion annual revenue run rates. (Amazon News) That makes Amazon unusually useful for testing the central hyperscaler debate: is enormous AI investment already pulling forward profitable cloud demand? First rejection is straightforward—if AWS acceleration fades while the FCF deficit persists, the thesis becomes “growth bought with capital” rather than operating leverage.
GOOGL — A, but normalize the denominator before calling it cheap. Core operating evidence is extremely strong: the pack has 23.1% revenue and 30.0% operating-profit growth, while Alphabet reported Cloud growth of 82%. (Investing.com) Yet I would not anchor on the pack's 17.2x P/E: the filing contains an enormous other-income contribution, so headline net income is not a clean recurring earnings denominator. At the same time, capex is 35.1% of revenue and proxy FCF yield just 1.27%; external reporting highlights the first negative quarterly FCF and a higher $195–205 billion 2026 capex plan. (Reuters) The wedge is compelling precisely because the rejection is so clear: normalize operating earnings and prove that Cloud economics compensate for the capital intensity. Until then, “17x Google” is potentially a denominator illusion.
MSFT — A, strongest proof of AI demand but entry-gated. The pack has 17.8% revenue growth, 20.8% operating-profit growth and 20.8% derived ROIC. The counterweight is a 1.79% FCF yield—at or below Microsoft's own historical 25th percentile—and a 28.9% one-month share-price gain. The latest operating read is exceptionally strong: FY26 Q4 Azure growth was 43%, commercial RPO rose 84% to $678 billion, and Microsoft said Microsoft 365 Copilot had passed 30 million paid seats. (Microsoft) So the variant wedge is not “AI works”; that increasingly looks consensus. It is whether capacity constraints and huge infrastructure commitments still generate enough incremental profit and cash return to support the re-rated stock. This is an entry/FCF-conversion gate, not an exposure-proof problem.
NVDA — A, event-gated into August 26. On pack fundamentals Nvidia is the cleanest growth/quality combination in the screen: 85.2% revenue growth, 147.4% operating-profit growth, 59.5% FCF margin and 78.9% derived ROIC, at 33.0x P/E. Its last reported quarter included 92% Data Center growth, and management guided Q2 FY27 revenue to $91 billion ±2% while assuming no China Data Center compute revenue. (NVIDIA Yatırımcı İlişkileri) The problem is freshness: those financials end April 26 and are 108 days old in the pack, while the estimated next earnings date is August 26. That makes the first rejection unusually clean: do not underwrite the long from stale hypergrowth data two weeks before a major reset of the denominator and expectations bar. It belongs immediately in an earnings-preview workflow.
Watchlist layer
ABBV has a credible replacement engine for Humira—Skyrizi and Rinvoq remained strong—and the Apogee acquisition adds immunology pipeline optionality. (Reuters) But the pack shows 19 downward FY EPS revisions in 30 days, and its proxy FCF yield sits at or below its own 25th percentile. I want the acquisition dilution and pipeline economics underwritten before advancing it.
META is the sharpest “great core business versus capital-allocation risk” watchlist name. Pack revenue growth is 30.4%, but gross, operating and net margin signals are adverse and FCF has weakened. Meta's Q2 release showed costs rising 55%, only $784 million of quarterly FCF, and a narrowed $130–145 billion 2026 capex range. (Atmeta) The first rejection is not user engagement or ads; it is whether incremental AI infrastructure earns an acceptable return.
NFLX has good underlying economics—26.7% pack FCF margin and 14.7% revenue growth after a 38% twelve-month share-price decline—but 30-day EPS revisions are net negative. Q3 guidance missed Wall Street expectations and advertising revenue came below expectations despite fast growth. (Investing.com) That creates an interesting recovery setup, but ads need to become a measurable second growth engine rather than a narrative bridge.
VZ is a genuine inflection candidate, but the filed financial trend has not caught up with the operating narrative. The pack still shows just 1.0% revenue growth, operating-profit decline of 4.5% and net-profit decline of 10%, although cash conversion is improving and P/E is 12.1x. Verizon subsequently highlighted 184,000 postpaid phone net adds, improved churn, higher cash flow, another guidance increase and an expanded buyback target. (Verizon) I want subscriber improvement to appear in recurring operating profit before upgrading it.
Screen flags only
AAPL has excellent underlying financial quality, but valuation and one-offs leave little variant room: 34.5x P/E, 29.0x EV/operating income—at or above its own 75th percentile—and only a 3.1% proxy FCF yield. Q3's 50.1% gross margin included roughly two percentage points from tariff refunds, making the headline margin less repeatable. (Yahoo Finans) China's new Qwen/Siri connection is strategically helpful, but not enough by itself to overcome the expectations bar. (Reuters)
CRM is a data-quality hold rather than a negative company view. Its pack valuation and FCF characteristics look interesting, but the pack explicitly flags the financial statements as 104 days old and says the market has a newer August 5 8-K that is not incorporated. I could not independently retrieve that newer print from the currently indexed Salesforce results page—the page I could access still exposed Q1 FY27 as the latest 2027 quarter—so I will not blend or invent newer numbers. (investor.salesforce.com) The correct action is to update the print first.
PEP looks inexpensive against its own history, but the apparent operating acceleration is lower quality than it first appears. The pack itself notes that reported operating-profit expansion benefited from prior-year impairments and acquisition/divestiture effects, while FY EPS has 18 downward revisions over 30 days. PepsiCo's external commentary also points to tighter North American consumer budgets, with 2026 organic-growth guidance still only 2–4%. (Fidelity) This needs organic volume and margin progression, not merely multiple support.
