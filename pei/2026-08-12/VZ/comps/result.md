# VZ — Comparable-Company Valuation

**Mode:** Report / screening comps
**Valuation date:** August 11, 2026 market data; August 12, 2026 consensus.
**Primary numeric source:** the uploaded deterministic comps pack. I have not substituted web-sourced financial or valuation figures. The pack and accompanying instructions both specify the `comps` workflow.  

## Valuation read

At **$47.27**, Verizon sits between two very different telecom valuation regimes:

* Against **AT&T**, its closest integrated-telecom economic comparison, VZ trades at a **56% P/E premium**, but only a **6.9% EV/operating-income premium**.
* Against **T-Mobile**, the wireless quality/growth reference, VZ trades at a **33% P/E discount**.
* Against the **cable/broadband convergence** median, VZ carries a **103% P/E premium** and a **36.9% EV/operating-income premium**. 

That makes the central conclusion less “VZ is cheap/expensive” and more: **the answer changes materially with the economic lens and denominator.** The AT&T comparison is particularly important because the huge P/E premium largely disappears at the enterprise operating level.

**Valuation posture: premium partly supported, but screening-only.** There is not enough peer breadth to support a clean standalone target price: the integrated and wireless lenses each contain only one numeric peer, while the broadband lens contains two.

## 1. Integrated telecom lens — AT&T

VZ trades at **12.14x P/E versus T at 7.79x**, a **56% premium**. That premium is not obviously explained by the supplied growth data: VZ's trailing revenue growth is 1.0% versus T's 2.6%, while consensus CY EPS growth is 6.4% versus 10.3%.

There are some quality offsets. VZ has a modestly higher operating margin, **22.45% vs. 21.72%**, and higher derived ROIC, **6.67% vs. 5.95%**. But those differences alone look too small to explain a 56% equity-earnings multiple premium. 

The enterprise measure tells a different story:

**12.66x VZ EV/operating income vs. 11.84x T = only a 6.9% premium.**

That is a much more defensible relative valuation given VZ's modest margin/ROIC advantage. It also means the P/E comparison is probably too sensitive to differences below operating income—capital structure, non-operating items, taxes or other denominator effects—to serve as the sole valuation anchor.

## 2. Wireless quality lens — T-Mobile

TMUS trades at **18.14x P/E**, versus VZ at 12.14x. VZ therefore trades at roughly a **33% discount** to the premium wireless operator. 

Here, however, the discount is supported by fundamentals:

* Revenue growth: **VZ 1.0% vs. TMUS 9.2%**
* ROIC: **6.67% vs. 8.61%**
* CY revenue growth consensus: **1.97% vs. 6.86%**
* CY EPS growth consensus: **6.42% vs. 12.61%**
* NY EPS growth consensus: **5.35% vs. 26.95%**

VZ actually has a slightly higher current operating margin, but the growth and return profile remains well below TMUS.

A mechanical rerating to TMUS's 18.14x P/E would imply about **$70.61/share, +49.4%**, but **the current fundamentals do not support using the full TMUS multiple as a base case**. It is more useful as an aspirational ceiling: that kind of rerating would require VZ to prove materially stronger growth and quality.

## 3. Broadband/mobile-convergence lens

The CHTR/CMCSA median is much cheaper:

* **5.97x P/E** versus VZ at 12.14x.
* **9.25x EV/operating income** versus VZ at 12.66x. 

Here VZ has a clearer fundamental case for a premium. It has:

* better revenue growth: **1.0% vs. 0.3%**;
* higher operating margin: **22.45% vs. 19.13%**;
* CY consensus revenue growth of **+1.97% vs. -1.72%**;
* CY EPS growth of **+6.42% vs. -1.33%**.

ROIC is roughly comparable at **6.67% vs. 6.89%**.

So a premium is economically reasonable. The open question is **how much**: VZ's 37% EV/operating-income premium and 103% P/E premium are substantial.

Mechanical broadband-median valuation produces roughly **$23–24/share**, around 50% below the current stock:

The consistency between those two outputs is notable, but I would **not** use that as VZ fair value: the cable peers have materially weaker growth expectations and a different operating mix. This lens is better used to test how much investors are paying for VZ's superior resilience rather than to set the primary multiple.

## What the current price implies

At **12.14x P/E**, the market is effectively valuing VZ:

**well above traditional integrated/cable telecom economics, but materially below a premium wireless-growth franchise.**

That valuation requires VZ to maintain enough operating quality to deserve a premium to T and cable, while the current multiple stops well short of assuming TMUS-like growth.

The most important tension is that **VZ's growth metrics presently do not justify a large premium to T on P/E**, whereas its operating margin and ROIC do support a **small enterprise-level premium**. In other words, **12.66x EV/operating income looks easier to defend than 12.14x P/E when VZ is compared directly with AT&T.**

## Rerating / derating paths

**Upside rerating:** VZ would need to move structurally toward the TMUS quality/growth profile—higher subscriber/service-revenue growth, better sustained EPS growth and improved returns on invested capital. Multiple expansion, rather than current earnings growth alone, would then become a substantial component of upside.

**Base/hold valuation regime:** Stable low-single-digit revenue growth plus modest EPS growth and sustained cash generation would argue for maintaining a premium to cable and perhaps a modest enterprise-value premium to T, but not a full TMUS multiple.

**Downside derating:** The first thing to break would be the premium to integrated telecom/cable if growth disappoints while margins or ROIC fail to improve. The **T EV/OI read-through around $41.65** is more economically coherent as a first derating reference than the much lower T P/E output, because the latter appears unusually sensitive to below-operating-line differences.

## PM conclusion

**VZ does not screen as straightforwardly cheap.** It is inexpensive relative to TMUS but has materially weaker growth and return characteristics; it is expensive relative to T and cable, although a portion of that premium is supported by stronger margins, growth versus cable and modestly better ROIC versus T.

The cleanest interpretation is:

> **VZ is priced for successful operational stabilization, but not yet for a TMUS-like growth outcome.**

The **integrated-telecom EV/operating-income lens is the most credible current anchor**, while TMUS is best treated as an upside/quality reference and CHTR/CMCSA as downside/convergence context. Because the core numeric peer set is sparse, a precise selected fair-value range is **not sufficiently supported for a new-money decision** from these comps alone. 

The next underwriting step would be to determine whether VZ's post-Frontier operating trajectory can close enough of the growth/ROIC gap to justify sustained multiple expansion; if a formal valuation range is needed, that is better paired with a DCF or a broader normalized peer set rather than forcing a target from four heterogeneous comps.
