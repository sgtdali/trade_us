# VZ — Long-Only Trade Pitch

**Source posture:** The uploaded pitch pack is the numeric source of truth; market/valuation data are as of **August 11, 2026** and consensus is as of **August 12, 2026**. The accompanying instructions and pack both specify the `pitch` workflow and a **long-only, common-equity-only** mandate.  

## 1. Trade Recommendation

**WATCHLIST / CONDITIONAL LONG — VZ common equity at $47.27 as of August 11, 2026, with a 6–12 month fundamental horizon.** The current illustrative scenario skew produces only **+8.3% probability-weighted price return through August 12, 2027**, excluding dividends, which is not enough to initiate today given the stock's recent rerating and its premium to AT&T. I would require either a better entry price or another quarter of operating proof. 

The setup is improving: 1H26 operating cash flow grew **9.9%**, cash conversion strengthened, management raised full-year guidance for the second consecutive quarter, and FY26 EPS revisions have turned positive. But the stock has already risen **10.75% over one month**, and FY26 EPS consensus has moved from **$4.941 thirty days ago to $5.012 today**, with 10 upward versus 2 downward revisions over that period. The early turnaround is therefore **not undiscovered**. 

## 2. Variant Perception

The potential long thesis is **not** simply “Verizon is cheap.” At 12.14x P/E, VZ is considerably more expensive than AT&T at 7.79x, although the enterprise-level gap is far smaller: **12.66x versus 11.84x EV/reported operating income**. Meanwhile, VZ remains well below TMUS's 18.14x P/E because its growth and ROIC profile remain materially weaker. 

The actual variant wedge would be:

> **Verizon's lower-churn, lower-subsidy operating model proves durable enough that today's stabilization becomes an earnings-and-cash-flow inflection, while the market continues to value VZ closer to legacy telecom than premium wireless.**

There is evidence for the first half of that thesis, but not enough yet for the second. Verizon reported **184,000 Q2 postpaid phone net additions**, said mobility and broadband service revenue grew **2.8%**, and expects that growth to approach **3.0% in Q3 and 4.0% in Q4**. Management characterizes the improvement as structural and tied to lower churn and healthier acquisition economics. ([Verizon][1])

### What is priced in

Current valuation appears to price **successful stabilization but not TMUS-like growth**. VZ's derived ROIC is 6.67%, versus 5.95% for T and 8.61% for TMUS; current-year consensus EPS growth is 6.42%, versus 10.27% and 12.61%, respectively. That supports some premium to legacy telecom, but not a premium-growth multiple. 

The other side of the debate is increasingly visible: SpaceX has articulated plans to expand Starlink toward a broader mobile service. Reuters reported that analysts see major execution and infrastructure hurdles to challenging the nationwide carriers over the next several years, but the announcement has nevertheless created a new structural valuation overhang for VZ, T and TMUS. ([Reuters][2])

## 4. Why Now / Catalyst Path

The next fundamental proof point is the **estimated October 20, 2026 Q3 earnings date**; the pack correctly marks that date as unconfirmed. 

Management's operating milestones are unusually concrete: mobility and broadband service revenue growth is expected to approach **3.0% in Q3 2026** and approximately **4.0% in Q4 2026**, versus 2.8% in Q2. FY26 adjusted EPS guidance is **$4.99–$5.04**, while the pack's issuer disclosure shows FCF guidance of **$21.94–22.14bn**. ([Verizon][1]) 

Two additional optionality buckets matter but should not yet be capitalized aggressively. Frontier became part of Verizon on **January 20, 2026**, creating a roughly 30 million-passing fiber footprint, while Verizon announced a **$1bn Google dark-fiber agreement** tied to data-center connectivity. Both create potential convergence/AI-infrastructure upside, but neither is sufficiently modeled in the supplied data to carry the valuation case today. ([Verizon][3])

## 6. Risk / Reward and Sizing Considerations

The favorable evidence is cash-flow conversion: operating cash flow rose 9.9% in 1H26, while management says reduced subsidies and lower churn are improving customer economics. The adverse evidence is that operating profit fell 4.5%, net profit fell 10.0%, and the deterministic signal set still flags operating-margin and net-margin trends as adverse. 

The biggest fundamental risks are therefore **turnaround durability**, **leverage**, **Frontier integration** and **competitive intensity**. The newer SpaceX debate adds long-duration technological risk; Reuters reports a genuine disagreement between investors concerned about disruption and analysts who believe building a competitive nationwide mobile service would require years of investment. ([Reuters][2])

Because no portfolio-level risk budget, volatility target or benchmark exists, I would not manufacture a percentage position size. If the idea clears the entry rules below, the appropriate posture is **starter exposure until Q4 2026 validates the 4.0% service-revenue growth objective**.

## 10. Open Items / Data Requests

The biggest missing item is the **earnings-call transcript**, so the pitch does not yet have enough detail on churn mechanics, Frontier synergy cadence, subsidy economics or management's sensitivity to competitive responses. Ownership concentration, passive/ETF ownership, short interest/crowding and factor exposures are also unavailable; those are relevant to volatility and sizing even for a long-only position.

A fuller valuation range would also benefit from a DCF or a broader normalized integrated-telecom peer set. The current four-company multi-lens framework is useful for framing the debate but too heterogeneous to justify a precise target.

### PM bottom line

**VZ is an improving company but not yet a compelling stock at $47.27.** The Q2 operating evidence is stronger, estimate revisions are moving the right way, and convergence/AI-fiber optionality is credible. But the market has already responded, and current expected price skew is only +8.3% before dividends. The better setup is to **wait for either $44.00 by September 30, 2026 with the thesis intact, or Q3 proof plus a price no higher than $46.00 on October 31, 2026.**
