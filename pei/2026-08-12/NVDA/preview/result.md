# NVDA — Q2 FY2027 Earnings Preview

**Audience:** long-only PM
**Print:** Wednesday, **August 26, 2026**; NVIDIA says results are due around 1:20 p.m. PT, followed by the call at 2:00 p.m. PT. This upgrades the pack’s previously estimated date to company-confirmed. ([NVIDIA Yatırımcı İlişkileri][1])

**Stance into the print: HOLD / WAIT FOR PROOF.** The fundamental backdrop remains exceptionally strong, but the expectation bar has risen with it. Consensus Q2 revenue of **$91.85B** is already **0.9% above NVIDIA’s $91.0B guide midpoint**, estimates have been revised upward, and the stock trades at roughly **33.0x P/E** on the pack’s valuation framework. A simple revenue beat is therefore unlikely to be enough; the more important hurdle is whether Q3 guidance validates the current **$103.13B** next-quarter consensus without sacrificing ~75% gross margin.

**No earnings implied move is available.** Options data, positioning, short interest and a sourced whisper are missing; I am therefore treating this as an earnings setup/reaction framework, not inventing an event-move hurdle.

## 3. Estimates are moving the wrong way for an easy setup

Current-quarter EPS consensus has risen from **$1.952 to $2.083 over 90 days**, about **+6.7%**. There have been **4 upward revisions and zero downward revisions in the last 30 days**. Full-year FY27 EPS has risen about **7.9%** over 90 days, while FY28 EPS has risen about **14.3%**. 

That creates a classic high-quality/high-bar setup: improving revisions support the fundamental thesis, but they also reduce the stock's tolerance for a merely in-line print. My inference is that the market is increasingly underwriting continuation of the beat-and-raise cadence rather than questioning whether AI infrastructure demand exists at all.

## 4. Last-quarter operating baseline

Q1 FY27 revenue was **$81.6B**, with Data Center revenue of **$75.2B**, up 92% YoY and 21% sequentially. Data Center therefore represented roughly **92% of total company revenue**. NVIDIA said hyperscalers were approximately half of Data Center revenue, with AI clouds, industrial, enterprise and sovereign customers making up the other half.  ([Securities and Exchange Commission][2])

The company had guided Q1 revenue to **$78B**, meaning the eventual $81.6B result was about **4.6% above the prior guide midpoint**. That history supports some degree of guidance conservatism, but the Q2 consensus has already absorbed part of that pattern. 

The supplied deterministic signals are overwhelmingly favorable across revenue, operating profit, FCF and margins. The notable adverse signal is **cash conversion**; Q1 operating cash flow was 86% of reported net profit. 

## 5. EPS is a secondary—and potentially misleading—bar

Q1 GAAP EPS was **$2.39** versus non-GAAP EPS of **$1.87**, and GAAP below-the-line income contained a very large investment-mark component. NVIDIA reported **$15.9B of net equity-security gains**, predominantly unrealized gains, while its non-GAAP framework excludes equity-security gains and losses from OI&E. ([Securities and Exchange Commission][3])

That makes the **$2.083 consensus EPS bar basis-sensitive**. The pack identifies the provider as Yahoo Finance analyst estimates but does not explicitly define whether every analyst contribution maps cleanly to NVIDIA's current non-GAAP convention. 

For the print, I would prioritize **revenue, gross margin, Q3 guidance and operating trajectory above headline GAAP EPS**. Any large equity-investment marks should be bridged out before interpreting earnings quality.

## 6. The five stock-moving debates

**First, how far above $91.85B does Q2 land?** Revenue above the $92.82B guide ceiling would be better evidence than a small consensus beat. A number well into the mid-$90Bs would materially strengthen the demand thesis.

**Second, Q3 guidance matters more than the backward-looking beat.** The current next-quarter consensus is **$103.13B**. A guide midpoint below that figure would create an uncomfortable “beat but decelerating expectations” setup; guidance at or above it would preserve the earnings-revision cycle. 

**Third, China has become upside optionality relative to the original guide.** NVIDIA's Q2 outlook explicitly assumed **no Data Center compute revenue from China**. U.S. policy now allows H200 license applications on a case-by-case basis, and Reuters reported that limited H200 shipments to China had begun by July 14. Because Q2 ended July 26, the key question is less whether a tiny amount entered Q2 and more **what China contribution management assumes in Q3 and beyond**. 

**Fourth, external AI infrastructure demand remains very strong.** Meta narrowed 2026 capex guidance to **$130B–$145B**; Oracle ended FY26 with **$638B of RPO**, much of the recent growth tied to large AI contracts; and CoreWeave just raised 2026 capex guidance to **$35B–$39B** amid strong AI demand. Microsoft has separately described capacity as constrained through 2026 despite very heavy investment. These are supportive demand read-throughs, though they do not translate mechanically into NVIDIA revenue. ([Atmeta][4])

**Fifth, the market is beginning to debate who finances the next leg.** The bullish case is that AI compute capacity continues to clear as fast as it can be built; the bearish counter is that the financing burden, depreciation and return requirements eventually discipline customer capex. NVIDIA's own push to secure long-term AI factory and HBM capacity reinforces how large the next infrastructure cycle could be—but also how capital-intensive it is. ([NVIDIA Yatırımcı İlişkileri][5])

## 7. Stock setup and valuation

At the pack's current valuation snapshot, NVIDIA carries roughly a **$5.26T market capitalization**, **33.0x P/E**, a **3.0% earnings yield** and a **2.27% parent-attributed FCF-yield proxy**. 

The shares were **+6.9% over one month**, approximately flat over three months, **+14.6% over six months** and **+19.2% over 12 months**, with ~36.7% annualized volatility. 

That is not a distressed setup. My inference is that **a good quarter is already partly priced in**: positive estimate revisions plus a premium multiple mean the market likely needs evidence that $100B+ quarterly revenue is becoming a durable floor rather than a temporary peak.

The analyst target distribution is extremely wide—**$180 to $500**, mean about **$303**—so price targets are not a useful earnings-event hurdle.

## 10. What would change sizing

For this **long-only** mandate, my pre-print posture is **HOLD / WAIT FOR PROOF rather than chase the event**. The supplied mandate permits holding through both weekly reviews and monthly rebalances and does not define a benchmark, so the decision should be framed on absolute thesis quality rather than index-relative weight. 

I would become more constructive if **Q3 guidance meets or exceeds the $103.13B consensus while gross margin remains around 75%**, particularly if the incremental growth is diversified across Hyperscale/ACIE and China is additive rather than required to make the guide.

I would reduce exposure if **forward revenue guidance misses consensus and the explanation is demand-related**, or if gross margin falls below the company's guided floor while management signals that product transition, supply costs or customer digestion will persist. That combination would challenge both the earnings trajectory and the premium valuation.

**The single most important number on August 26 is therefore not Q2 EPS. It is the Q3 revenue guide midpoint.**
